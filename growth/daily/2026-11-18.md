### Hindi SRT Proofreading: A Practical Checklist Before Export

A Hindi subtitle file should match the spoken audio—not just look correct when read silently. Names, numbers, mixed-language phrases and timing can all change the meaning of a caption. Treat an automatic transcript as a first draft and review it before exporting the SRT file.

#### 1. Choose the writing system deliberately

Decide whether the video should use Devanagari Hindi or Roman Hinglish. Follow the audience and the way the speaker naturally communicates. Avoid switching scripts randomly within the same sentence unless the mixed style is intentional.

#### 2. Verify names, places and numbers

Speech-recognition drafts can misread proper names, locations, dates and quantities. Replay the relevant sentence and compare the caption with the audio. Use the spelling supplied by the speaker or the verified source material.

#### 3. Keep the meaning and punctuation

Correct missing words, repeated words and punctuation that changes the sentence. Do not rewrite the speaker's meaning just to make a caption shorter. If a sentence is too long, divide it at a natural pause.

#### 4. Check line breaks on a phone screen

A caption that looks comfortable on a desktop may be difficult to read on a phone. Keep each cue concise, avoid awkward breaks between a name and its title, and make sure important visual details are not covered by the caption placement.

#### 5. Verify SRT timing

An SRT cue has a sequence number, start time, end time and caption text. Check that each cue appears with the spoken words and disappears before the next thought becomes confusing. Replay any line that appears too early, too late or for too short a time.

#### 6. Preview the exported file

After correcting the transcript and timing, export the SRT and preview it with the video. Check the beginning, middle and end—not just the first caption. If a correction changes the timing, review the neighbouring cues as well.

#### Final review

Before using the subtitle file, confirm that the words match the audio, the chosen script is consistent, line breaks are readable and timestamps are accurate. A careful final preview is more reliable than assuming an automatic transcript is perfect.
