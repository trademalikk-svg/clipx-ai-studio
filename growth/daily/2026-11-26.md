## 1. Product-truth guardrails

All content below reflects the current repository implementation:

- Manual Clip Cutter: the user chooses exact start and end times.
- Automatic Shorts Maker: creates consecutive, fixed-duration segments. It is not semantic AI highlight detection and does not decide which moments are interesting.
- Clip processing and source-video preview run in the browser/on the user's device; cloud rendering is disabled in Free Browser Mode.
- Caption Studio can draft multilingual captions with an on-device Whisper model in supported browsers/devices. Draft text and timings must be reviewed. Manual captions can be exported as SRT.
- Thumbnail Studio uses the user's own image and text on a 1280 × 720 browser canvas with five visual presets. It is template-based, not an AI image generator.
- Video Prompt Builder creates and copies text prompts only. It does not generate video or call a paid video API.
- Creator Kit titles/hashtags are local-template suggestions, not researched or generative-AI outputs.

Do not claim guaranteed virality, automatic story understanding, cloud rendering, automatic best-moment selection, AI thumbnail generation, or video generation inside the prompt builder.

### H1: 9:16 vs 16:9 vs 1:1 — Which Video Aspect Ratio Should You Choose?

The aspect ratio describes the shape of your video frame. Choosing it before editing helps you plan where the subject, captions and important action should appear. ClipX Clip Settings currently offers three choices: 9:16 Shorts, 16:9 Landscape and 1:1 Square.

The right choice depends on the destination and the footage. Always check the current upload requirements of the platform where you plan to post; platform specifications can change.

### 1. 9:16 — Vertical video

A 9:16 frame is tall and narrow. It is commonly used for mobile-first short-form video, including YouTube Shorts and vertical Reels. Keep the main person or object near the centre, leave enough room for captions, and avoid placing important text too close to the edges.

Before exporting, preview the whole clip on a phone-sized screen. A crop that looks fine on a desktop may hide a face, product or subtitle on a smaller display.

### 2. 16:9 — Landscape video

A 16:9 frame is wide. It is a familiar format for standard landscape YouTube videos, tutorials, demonstrations and presentations. It gives more horizontal room for two people, a desktop screen or a wide scene.

Do not stretch a vertical source to fill a landscape frame. Choose a crop or background treatment that preserves the important subject, and check that text remains readable.

### 3. 1:1 — Square video

A 1:1 frame is square. It can be useful when a square layout suits the platform placement or when the composition is centred. It is not a universal replacement for vertical or landscape formats.

Keep the main subject within the central area and preview the final crop before export.

### 4. Choose the ratio before adding captions

If captions are placed too close to the edge, changing the frame later may crop them. Choose the output ratio first, then position subtitles and titles with enough breathing room. Review names, numbers and timing before publishing.

### 5. Test a short section before exporting the full video

Large video files can take longer to load and export. ClipX browser processing depends on your device, browser memory and codec support; cloud rendering is disabled in the free browser workflow. Test a short sample first, then review the exported file from beginning to end.

### Quick checklist

- Match the frame to the intended destination.
- Keep the important subject inside the safe central area.
- Do not stretch footage to fill a different shape.
- Leave room for captions and platform interface overlays.
- Preview on a phone-sized screen.
- Check the destination platform's current format rules.
- Review the exported clip before publishing.

### Frequently asked questions

**Is 9:16 the only format for Shorts and Reels?**  
It is the vertical format shown in ClipX for Shorts. Check each destination platform's current specifications before upload.

**Can I turn a 16:9 video into 9:16 without losing anything?**  
A different frame shape usually requires cropping or a layout choice. Review the crop so that important visual information is not cut off.

**Does ClipX automatically choose the best aspect ratio?**  
No. The user selects the ratio in Clip Settings. Preview the result and adjust the source framing as needed.

**Does changing the ratio make a video go viral?**  
No. Format is only one production choice; it does not guarantee views or engagement.
