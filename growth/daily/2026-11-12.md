### Article body

# YouTube Shorts Series: How to Plan Seasons and Episodes

A connected set of short videos can make a topic easier to follow. Instead of placing every detail in one clip, creators can divide a subject into clear episodes. The important part is that each video should still provide value to someone who discovers it first.

YouTube announced Shorts Series in September 2026. The feature lets creators organize Shorts into seasons and episodes, with custom thumbnails and sequential playback. YouTube said the rollout was beginning across web, mobile and TV. The available controls can vary by account, so check the current options in YouTube Studio before planning around a specific feature.

## 1. Start with one audience question

Choose a topic that can be explained in a few connected steps. Write down the main question a viewer wants answered. If the topic cannot be divided naturally, a series may not be necessary.

## 2. Give every episode a clear role

A simple three-part structure is:

- Episode 1: introduce the question and explain what the viewer will learn.
- Episode 2: show one useful step or explain the central idea.
- Episode 3: provide an example, answer a common follow-up and summarize the lesson.

This is an editorial planning template, not a YouTube requirement. Adjust the number of episodes to the subject.

## 3. Make every Short useful on its own

Avoid making an episode only a teaser for the next one. Add enough context for a new viewer to understand the point. A short reminder of the series topic can help, but the main explanation should happen in the current video.

## 4. Use consistent titles and visual cues

Keep the series name, episode wording and visual style consistent. Custom thumbnails can help distinguish episodes where the feature is available. Use clear, readable text and avoid putting important details too close to the screen edges.

## 5. Organize the viewing order in YouTube

If Shorts Series controls are available in your YouTube Studio, arrange the videos into the appropriate season and episode order. Review sequential playback and confirm that the next episode follows naturally. The series setup and publishing are YouTube actions; a video editor only prepares the individual files.

## 6. Review rights and AI disclosure before publishing

Check that footage, music and other material are cleared for use. If a video contains photorealistic or meaningfully altered/generated content, review YouTube's current disclosure requirements. YouTube's May 2026 guidance says the disclosure label for applicable Shorts appears as an overlay. The treatment can differ for unrealistic or animated content, so use the current upload screen and official Help guidance rather than assuming every AI-assisted edit is treated identically.

## Final checklist

- One clear audience question.
- A useful role for every episode.
- Each Short makes sense on its own.
- Consistent series naming and readable thumbnails.
- Episode order checked in YouTube Studio if Shorts Series is available.
- Footage and audio rights reviewed.
- AI disclosure controls checked where applicable.
- Every exported file played from beginning to end.
