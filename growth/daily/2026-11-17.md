### Hindi Subtitles for Shorts: A Practical SRT Quality Checklist

Automatic transcription can save the first round of typing, but the transcript still needs a careful review. Hindi speech often includes names, places, English product terms, numbers and code-switched phrases. A small transcription error can change the meaning of a sentence or make a subtitle difficult to follow.

Use this checklist before exporting an SRT file.

#### 1. Replay unclear words

Listen to the original audio whenever a word looks unusual or incomplete. Pay extra attention to names, place names, technical terms and words spoken quickly. Do not guess a spelling just because it looks familiar. If the audio is unclear, replay that segment or leave a note for a later review.

#### 2. Review Hindi and English mixed speech

Many Hindi videos naturally switch between Devanagari and English words. Keep the wording consistent with the speaker and the audience. Check product names, app names, abbreviations and numbers carefully. Avoid changing a speaker's intended phrase just to make the sentence look more formal.

#### 3. Correct punctuation and line breaks

Use punctuation to show where a thought ends or pauses. Break long captions into readable phrases instead of placing a full paragraph on one screen. Preview the subtitle on a phone-sized display and make sure the text does not cover a face, important object or platform interface.

#### 4. Match every caption to the audio

Check that each subtitle appears when the words are spoken and disappears before the next thought becomes confusing. Replay transitions between captions, because timing errors often appear where one line ends and the next begins. A transcript can be spelled correctly and still feel wrong if its timing is late or early.

#### 5. Inspect the exported SRT

Open the downloaded SRT in a text editor or subtitle tool. Confirm that entries are numbered, timestamps are present and the text is readable. Then play the complete video with subtitles enabled. If you edit the transcript again, export a fresh file and repeat the final check.

#### 6. Choose the right caption format

Keep the SRT separate when you want selectable subtitles in a supported player or platform workflow. Use burned-in captions when you need the text permanently visible in the video. The two formats serve different purposes, so choose before export.

#### Final review

A reliable subtitle workflow is: transcribe, correct, time, export and replay. Browser speech recognition can make mistakes or fail on a device with limited memory, so keep the original audio available for checking.
