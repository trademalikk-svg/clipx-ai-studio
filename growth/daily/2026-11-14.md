**Working title:** YouTube AI Disclosure for Shorts: When to Label Realistic AI Content in 2026
**Target:** Expand the existing guide-youtube-shorts.html section; no new URL.
**Meta description proposal (not applied):** Learn when YouTube asks creators to disclose realistic AI-generated or meaningfully altered Shorts, which edits are generally exempt, and where to check the setting.
**H1 proposal (not applied):** YouTube AI Disclosure for Shorts: Realistic vs Non-Realistic Content
**Suggested H2s:**
1. When YouTube expects disclosure
2. Examples of realistic AI-generated or altered content
3. What generally does not need disclosure
4. Where the Shorts label appears
5. A pre-upload review checklist

**Article copy:**

YouTube's altered-content disclosure is intended to give viewers context when a video presents realistic synthetic or meaningfully altered material. It does not mean that every use of an AI-assisted tool needs a label.

### When YouTube expects disclosure
Creators should disclose content that appears realistic and could make viewers believe that something happened when it did not. Examples include making a real person appear to say or do something they never said or did, changing footage of a real event or place, or generating a realistic scene that never occurred.

### What generally does not need the same disclosure
Clearly unrealistic animation or fantasy scenes and minor aesthetic edits that do not change what happened generally do not need disclosure. A script outline, a simple colour adjustment, or an obvious cartoon effect should not be confused with realistic synthetic footage. Review the official examples because context matters.

### Where the label appears
YouTube's May 2026 creator update says disclosure for photorealistic and meaningfully AI-altered or generated content can appear as an on-video overlay on Shorts. YouTube may also apply labels automatically when its systems identify eligible content. Check YouTube Studio and the current Help page before publishing.

### Does the label itself reduce reach?
YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit a video's audience or its eligibility to earn money. This is not a guarantee of views, recommendations or revenue. The video still needs to comply with Community Guidelines and provide value to viewers.

### Pre-upload checklist
- Does the final video look like real footage?
- Does it make a real person appear to say or do something they did not?
- Does it alter a real event or place?
- Does it create a realistic scene that never happened?
- Have you answered YouTube Studio's disclosure question accurately?
- Have you checked the current official guidance?

**Official source:** https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en  
**Label update:** https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/
